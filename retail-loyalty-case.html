Case Study | Retail Loyalty Data Pipeline

Transforming messy Sari-sari Store transactions and loyalty-customer records into validated, dashboard-ready data using Databricks SQL.

Databricks SQL Data Cleaning Data Quality Business Analytics

Project Overview

This end-to-end pipeline processes two source datasets: retail transaction details and loyalty-cardholder records. Databricks SQL moves the data through Bronze, Silver, and Gold layers, with validation and dashboard queries completing the workflow.

The project focuses on making imperfect source data reliable enough for customer, retailer, product-brand, and monthly purchasing analysis.

Business or Analytical Question

How does purchasing behavior differ by customer age group, retailer, product brand, and month?

The Gold layer is designed to answer this question through reusable measures for sales, units purchased, transactions, customers, and average transaction value.

Data Cleaning and Validation

Transaction records with zero or negative quantities or sales values were removed. Retailer and product-brand names were standardized, duplicates were handled, and invalid loyalty birthdays were excluded after date parsing.

  • 3,167 transaction records reduced to 3,161 clean records.
  • 5,949 loyalty records reduced to 5,945 clean records.
  • 6 invalid transactions and 4 invalid birthdays were removed.
  • Validation checks confirmed no missing customer or transaction IDs.

Gold-Layer Analysis

The business-ready table derives customer age, age group, transaction month, customer-retailer relationships, and customer-brand relationships.

Dashboard queries support monthly sales trends, sales by age group and retailer, top product brands, heatmaps, and average spend per transaction.

Key Outputs or Findings

Reliable data

Cleaned and validated tables with traceable source-to-output record counts.

Business measures

Sales, units, transactions, customers, and average transaction value.

Dashboard-ready views

Reusable queries for age, retailer, brand, and monthly purchasing patterns.
